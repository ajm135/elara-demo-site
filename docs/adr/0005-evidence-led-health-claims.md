# 0005: Evidence-led health claims

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-18

## Context

The 18 October 2026 innovation review of the Demo Site found that its health content had no evidence trail: the copy implied AI would eventually cure cancer and many other diseases, and presented an average human lifespan of about 110–120 years within a couple of generations, with no citations, identified publications, or review dates. It also emphasized benefits without their trade-offs (false positives, missed diagnoses, overdiagnosis, uneven performance across populations) and gave readers no clear way to tell clinical use from experimental research or speculation.

Milestone 2 responded by extending the static content model in `src/lib/site-content.ts` with a small catalogue of real, inspectable publications and consequential claims carrying an evidence stage and a last-reviewed date; by replacing the unsupported copy; and by rendering each claim's evidence beside the claim itself (`src/components/claim-evidence.tsx`). This ADR records the resulting content policy of record.

## Decision

- Every consequential health claim on the five pages is linked in `src/lib/site-content.ts` to one or more real publications in `referenceCatalogue`, each identified by title, publishing body or journal, publication date, and exactly one stable https URL or DOI. No claim ships that is not tied to a real, inspectable source.
- Every consequential claim carries exactly one of three evidence stages — clinical use, experimental research, or speculation — and a `lastReviewed` date that records when its sources were actually inspected. A null date means "not yet inspected" and renders as an explicit "not yet re-checked" gap; tests reject shipped claims that have no real inspection date.
- The specific finding each claim relies on, where that finding appears in the source, what the finding does not support, and the claim's limitations are recorded per claim in `docs/runbooks/content-evidence.md` (the claim evidence register). That register states, as policy, that clinical use is not universal availability or approval: an approved, in-use device is approved for a specific task in a specific place, not everywhere, and not for every use.
- Unsupported cure predictions ("cure cancer and many other diseases") and 110–120-year population-average lifespan forecasts are removed from shipped copy, route metadata, and project guidance, and are not retained anywhere to satisfy old tests: the obsolete assertions were replaced, not preserved. No future population-average lifespan forecast is made, and no cure is promised.
- No researcher attributions that cannot be verified are shipped: the copy uses no "some/many researchers expect" framing. Where a peer-reviewed source backs a claim, it is named by publication title, journal, and DOI in the register and rendered beside the claim — not by individual researchers.
- The longevity page explains the three lifespan distinctions: life expectancy (a population average), maximum lifespan (the longest individual lives on record, a fact about individuals, not a population forecast), and healthy lifespan (the proportion of life spent in good health).
- Every medical story distinguishes clinical use, experimental research, and speculation in the copy, and explains the relevant trade-offs beside the benefits — false positives, missed diagnoses, overdiagnosis, uneven performance across populations, and clinician oversight — while keeping the hopeful, non-promising tone and the per-page disclaimers.
- This ADR supersedes the conflicting medical assertions in ADR 0002, ADR 0003, and ADR 0004: the statements that the site's story is "how AI is expected to help humanity diagnose, treat, and eventually cure cancer and many other diseases", that "some researchers expect average human lifespan to reach about 110–120 years", and that the copy "presents cures and a 110-120 year average lifespan as expectations held by some researchers, not as certainties" no longer describe the shipped content, and this ADR is the policy of record for the health content instead. It also supersedes any obsolete sentence of the same kind in `docs/runbooks/local-start.md`; at the date of this ADR, no such sentence remains in that runbook (its "A note about the content" already matches this policy).
- This ADR changes nothing in the hosting, browser, or accessibility decisions of the superseded ADRs. The static content model (ADR 0002), the five illustrated App Router routes and their local-only rendering (ADR 0003), and no sign-in, no database, loopback-only local hosting with its verification, WCAG 2.2 Level AA, and current Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari support on phone, tablet, and desktop (ADR 0004) all remain in force, as does ADR 0001's stack. Only the medical-assertion text is superseded; each of those ADRs carries a short superseded-by note pointing to this one.
- The rendered evidence blocks are plain semantic markup with visible link text; the citation anchors point only to the publications' own stable https pages or DOI resolvers, and rendering never requires remote assets, live data, or JavaScript, so the site stays static, local-only, and readable without JavaScript.

## Notes

- This ADR makes no claim of browser-level or WCAG verification: browser and assistive-technology checks remain unverified as recorded in the innovation review and the project's explicit verification gaps, and the WCAG 2.2 AA and browser-support requirements of ADR 0004 stand unchanged as requirements, not as completed checks.
- The register, not this ADR, is where the source-by-source findings live; this ADR is the policy of record for how health claims are made, staged, dated, and reviewed on the site.
- Tests: `tests/claim-references.test.ts` covers reference integrity, required publication metadata, evidence stages, real review dates, and claim-to-sentence attachment; `tests/claim-evidence-rendering.test.tsx` covers the rendered evidence beside each consequential claim, route metadata in sync with the copy, and the absence of the obsolete forecast strings.
